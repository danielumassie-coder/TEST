# Methodological Guidelines for Relational Research

## Research Design Philosophy

### Abductive Approach
Move iteratively between:
- **Empirical observations**: What you see in the field
- **Theoretical concepts**: How you make sense of it
- **Novel insights**: What emerges from the dialogue

This is not:
- Pure induction (let data speak for itself)
- Pure deduction (test pre-specified hypotheses)

But rather:
- Abduction (iterate between data and theory to generate new understanding)

### Ethnographic Immersion

#### Deep Engagement
- Extended time in the field (typically 12-24 months)
- Multiple data sources (observation, interviews, documents, artifacts)
- Participation where appropriate to gain insider understanding
- Reflexive field noting that captures both observation and interpretation

#### What Deep Means
Not just:
- Showing up occasionally
- Conducting interviews only
- Observing from a distance

But rather:
- Being present during mundane and critical moments
- Developing relationships that enable access
- Understanding context deeply enough to theorize

### Every Example Does Theoretical Work

#### The Principle
No "mere description"—every empirical detail serves conceptual purpose

#### In Practice
When including an empirical example, ask:
- What theoretical point does this illustrate?
- What concept does this help develop?
- What mechanism does this reveal?

If you can't answer these questions, cut the example.

## Data Collection

### Observational Data
**What to capture:**
- Micro-interactions and their relational dynamics
- Moments of breakdown and repair
- Practices that build or erode infrastructure
- Language use and framing shifts
- Embodied and material aspects of collaboration

**How to capture:**
- Real-time field notes during observation
- Expanded notes within 24 hours
- Memo writing to develop emerging insights
- Selective recording (audio/video) when appropriate and permitted

### Interview Data
**Purpose:**
- Not just getting people's accounts
- But understanding their sense-making
- And probing theoretical hunches

**Approach:**
- Semi-structured to allow emergence
- Iterative interview guides that evolve
- Theoretical sampling (interview people who can illuminate emerging concepts)
- Member reflections (share emerging insights for feedback)

### Documentary Data
**Sources:**
- Meeting notes and agendas
- Internal communications
- Public documents
- Artifacts and visual materials

**Use:**
- Triangulation with observational data
- Understanding formal structures and processes
- Tracking changes over time
- Revealing espoused vs. enacted approaches

## Data Analysis

### Coding Process
1. **Open coding**: Identify phenomena in the data
2. **Focused coding**: Develop categories and relationships
3. **Theoretical coding**: Connect to broader concepts and theory
4. **Comparative analysis**: Look for patterns and variations

### Abductive Iteration
Throughout analysis:
- Move between field notes and theoretical literature
- Let empirical patterns challenge theoretical assumptions
- Use theory to see patterns in empirics
- Generate novel theoretical insights from the dialogue

### Analytical Strategies

#### Pattern Recognition
Look for:
- Recurring practices across cases
- Variations that illuminate mechanisms
- Breakdowns that reveal normally invisible infrastructure
- Temporal sequences and processes

#### Comparative Analysis
Compare:
- Successful vs. unsuccessful collaborations
- Different contexts (healthcare, climate, etc.)
- Early vs. late stages of initiatives
- Different actor groups' approaches

#### Theoretical Sampling
As patterns emerge:
- Seek cases that test emerging theory
- Interview people who can illuminate concepts
- Observe situations that should reveal mechanisms
- Look for negative cases that challenge claims

## Establishing Rigor

### Credibility
- Prolonged engagement in field
- Persistent observation across multiple sites/moments
- Triangulation of data sources
- Member checking of interpretations

### Transferability
- Thick description of context
- Clear articulation of theoretical mechanisms
- Framework applicable across contexts
- Explicit about scope conditions

### Dependability
- Detailed audit trail of analytical decisions
- Transparent about researcher positionality
- Clear documentation of methods
- Replicable analytical procedures

### Confirmability
- Evidence grounded in data
- Alternative explanations considered
- Disconfirming evidence addressed
- Reflexivity about researcher influence

## Reflexivity

### Researcher Positionality
Be explicit about:
- Your background and how it shapes what you see
- Your relationships with research participants
- Your theoretical commitments and potential biases
- How your presence may influence the setting

### Without Navel-Gazing
**DO**:
- Acknowledge how your position shapes the research
- Use reflexivity to strengthen interpretation
- Be transparent about limitations

**DON'T**:
- Make the research about you
- Endless introspection without analytical purpose
- Apologize for having a perspective

### Reflexive Practices
- Regular memo writing about your analytical process
- Peer debriefing to test interpretations
- Acknowledge when you're surprised by data (reveals assumptions)
- Track how your understanding evolves

## Visual Representation

### Process Models
Show:
- Temporal sequences of practices
- Feedback loops and recursive patterns
- Critical junctures and branching paths
- How micro-practices aggregate to macro-outcomes

### Relational Maps
Depict:
- Networks of relationships
- Power dynamics
- Information flows
- Boundary spanning

### Conceptual Frameworks
Illustrate:
- Relationships between concepts
- Mechanisms linking phenomena
- Levels of analysis
- Theoretical contributions

### Design Principles
- Clarity over complexity
- Conceptual purpose over decoration
- Labels that do theoretical work
- Visual metaphors that illuminate

## Writing About Methods

### In Methods Section
**Include:**
- Setting and access
- Data sources and collection procedures
- Analytical approach
- Limitations and how you addressed them

**Be specific enough for:**
- Others to evaluate your approach
- Others to build on your methods
- Readers to assess credibility

**Don't:**
- Provide excessive methodological justification
- Defensive posturing about qualitative methods
- Boilerplate about grounded theory

### Throughout the Paper
**Show methodological choices:**
- How you selected empirical examples
- Why you focus on particular moments
- How you arrived at theoretical interpretations

**But don't:**
- Interrupt the argument with methods talk
- Over-explain every analytical decision
- Make methods the main story

## Common Methodological Pitfalls

### Avoid
- Treating ethnography as mere description
- Coding without theoretical purpose
- Letting data "speak for itself" without interpretation
- Over-claiming from limited data
- Under-using rich data to support claims
- Separating methods from theoretical contribution

### Embrace
- Using methods to generate theoretical insight
- Tight integration of empirical evidence and conceptual development
- Strategic selection of exemplars that do theoretical work
- Transparent about how you moved from data to claims
- Methods that others can build on and extend
