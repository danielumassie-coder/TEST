# Domain-Specific Guidelines: Collaborative Innovation and Interdependent Problem-Solving

## Core Framing

### Collaboration as Existential Challenge
**Not just a coordination problem**

Collaboration involves:
- **Identity** challenges (Who am I in this collaboration?)
- **Meaning** challenges (What does this work mean?)
- **Security** challenges (Am I safe to be vulnerable?)

### Beyond Coordination
Don't reduce collaboration to:
- Information sharing
- Task allocation
- Workflow management

Instead, recognize it as work that:
- Challenges fundamental assumptions
- Requires ontological security
- Demands moral coherence
- Depends on solidarity

## Understanding Conflict

### "Stupid Destructive Conflicts"
These stem from:
- **Ontological insecurity**: Fundamental uncertainty about one's place and role
- **Moral incoherence**: Conflicting values or ethical frameworks
- **Solidarity deficits**: Lack of relational foundation for trust

### Reframing Conflict
- Not just disagreement about facts or preferences
- Deeper struggles over meaning, identity, and belonging
- Symptoms of relational infrastructure failure

## Win-Win as Relational Work

### Challenge the Easy Framing
**Win-win emerges from relational work, not just win-win framing**

- It's not about clever negotiation tactics
- It's not about finding hidden value
- It's about doing the hard relational work

### What the Work Entails
- Building ontological security
- Establishing moral coherence
- Creating solidarity
- Enabling genuine co-creation

## Micro-Macro Connections

### Connecting Practices to Institutions
Show how:
- Micro-practices aggregate to macro-patterns

## Literature Bridging

### Span Multiple Domains
Connect:
- **Public management** literature

### Show Integration
- Don't treat these as separate silos
- Show how insights from one domain illuminate another
- Build cumulative knowledge across boundaries

## The Relational Turn

### Core Argument
Most organizational theory:
- Atomizes actors
- Treats relationships as secondary
- Misses the relational constitution of action

Your work:
- Centers relationships
- Shows how actors are constituted through relations
- Reveals relational dynamics as primary

### Implications
- Different unit of analysis (dyads, networks, not individuals)
- Different causal stories (relational processes, not individual choices)
- Different interventions (relational infrastructure, not individual incentives)

## Emergent Value Creation

### The 1+1>2 Question
How do actors create emergent value together?

Not through:
- Additive combination of individual contributions
- Pre-specified synergies
- Optimization of known resources

But through:
- Genuine co-creation
- Emergent possibilities
- Relational transformation

### Showing Emergence
- Document moments when something new appears
- Show how relations enable what individuals couldn't do
- Reveal the generative power of connection

## Existential Infrastructure

### The Concept
The underlying relational and meaning-making systems that make collaboration possible

Includes:
- **Trust foundations**: How actors establish reliability
- **Meaning coherence**: How shared understanding emerges
- **Identity security**: How actors maintain stable sense of self
- **Moral legitimacy**: How actors justify actions to each other

### Why It Matters
- Usually invisible until it fails
- Requires active maintenance
- Can be designed and built
- Shapes what's possible in collaboration

### Studying It
- Look for breakdown moments
- Examine repair work
- Notice what makes collaboration flow
- Identify practices that build or erode infrastructure
