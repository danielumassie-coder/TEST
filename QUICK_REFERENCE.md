# Quick Reference Card

## Writing Formula

### Opening (3-4 paragraphs)
1. Provocative puzzle (2-3 sentences)
2. What this challenges (2-3 sentences)
3. Your theoretical lens (2-3 sentences)
4. Three-level contribution preview (3-4 sentences)

### Body
- **Theory**: Argumentative integration (not citation lists)
- **Empirics**: Strategic exemplars (scene + moment + interpretation)
- **Analysis**: Alternate close reading and pattern abstraction

### Discussion
1. Bold claim
2. Evidence summary
3. Theoretical implications
4. Counterintuitive insight

### Implications
- **Practitioners**: Actionable framework that challenges conventional wisdom
- **Scholars**: Research agenda opening new territory
- **Policy**: Design principles for relational infrastructure

## Three-Level Contribution Template

**Empirical**: This study reveals [specific phenomenon] through [method/setting].

**Theoretical**: These findings challenge [existing theory] by showing [new relationship/mechanism], extending our understanding of [core construct].

**Practical**: Actors can [specific action] by [framework/approach], enabling [outcome] across [contexts].

---

## Sentence-Level Patterns

### Strong Lead Sentences
❌ "Prior research has examined collaboration in various contexts."
✅ "Collaboration requires existential, not just coordination, infrastructure."

### Active vs. Passive
❌ "It was found that actors engaged in relational work."
✅ "Actors built ontological security through daily micro-practices."

### Citation Integration
❌ "Many scholars have studied this topic (A, 2010; B, 2012; C, 2015)."
✅ "Dominant coordination frameworks (A, 2010; B, 2012) cannot explain why well-designed collaborations fail—a puzzle I address by examining existential infrastructure (C, 2015)."

### Theory-Empirics Connection
❌ "Sarah said, 'I think we should try a different approach.'"
✅ "Sarah's proposal—'What if we connect metrics to lived experience?'—illustrates ontological bridging: linking different ways of knowing to create new possibilities."

## Common Pitfalls - Quick Checks

### ❌ AVOID
- Literature review windups (>2 pages before your argument)
- Citation strings without argument
- Theory and data in separate silos
- Buried insights (save good stuff for discussion)
- Apologetic framing ("this small study")
- Generic implications ("future research needed")
- Pure description without theoretical purpose

### ✅ EMBRACE
- Counterintuitive opening
- Citations building arguments
- Theory woven through empirics
- Leading with key insights
- Bold contribution claims
- Specific, challenging implications
- Every detail doing conceptual work

## Red Flag Self-Test

If you answer YES to any of these, revise:

1. Does my opening spend >2 paragraphs reviewing literature before getting to my puzzle?
2. Do I have theory sections that could fit any paper on this topic?
3. Are my empirical examples purely descriptive without theoretical interpretation?
4. Are my most important insights buried in the middle of the paper?
5. Do my implications read like generic boilerplate?
6. Am I treating micro and macro as separate levels?
7. Is my voice predominantly passive and heavily hedged?
