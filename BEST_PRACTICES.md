# Common Pitfalls to Avoid and Best Practices to Embrace

## AVOID

### In Literature Review
❌ **Excessive literature review windups**
- Don't spend pages cataloging what everyone has said
- Avoid literature review for its own sake
- Don't delay getting to your argument

❌ **Listing citations like credentials**
- Citations are not a badge of erudition
- Don't cite just to show you've read something
- Avoid strings of citations without argument

### In Theory-Empirics Relationship
❌ **Separating theory from empirics artificially**
- Don't have standalone "theory" and "findings" sections that don't connect
- Avoid theory that doesn't illuminate your empirics
- Don't present findings without theoretical interpretation

### In Contribution Framing
❌ **Burying key insights in middle sections**
- Don't hide your most important findings
- Avoid saving the good stuff for the discussion
- Don't make readers hunt for your contribution

❌ **Apologetic framing of contributions**
- Don't downplay the importance of your work
- Avoid excessive hedging of claims
- Don't say "this small study suggests..."

### In Implications
❌ **Generic policy implications**
- Avoid boilerplate about "future research needed"
- Don't suggest vague "managerial implications"
- Don't offer advice that could apply to any study

### In Empirical Presentation
❌ **Purely descriptive ethnography**
- Don't just describe what you saw
- Avoid ethnography without theoretical purpose
- Don't let description overwhelm analysis

## EMBRACE

### In Opening
✅ **Opening with counterintuitive findings**
- Start with something that challenges expectations
- Lead with a puzzle that matters
- Hook readers with intellectual surprise

### In Theory Building
✅ **Theorizing from thick empirical moments**
- Use rich empirical details to build theory
- Show how specific moments reveal general principles
- Let empirics do theoretical work

### In Visualization
✅ **Visual models showing process/relationships**
- Create figures that map relational dynamics
- Show temporal processes visually
- Use diagrams to clarify complex relationships

### In Scholarly Dialogue
✅ **Explicit dialogue with canonical texts**
- Engage directly with foundational work
- Show what classic theories miss or get wrong
- Build on shoulders of giants while pushing forward

### In Closing
✅ **Bold closing provocations**
- End with strong claims that matter
- Challenge readers to think differently
- Leave them with something memorable

### In Practice
✅ **Practical frameworks that challenge conventional wisdom**
- Offer actionable guidance that's not obvious
- Provide frameworks practitioners can use
- Challenge standard management practices

### In Methods
✅ **Methodology that others can build on**
- Describe methods others can replicate
- Share methodological innovations
- Make your approach transparent and transportable

## Specific Traps in Your Domain

### When Writing About Collaboration
**AVOID**: Reducing collaboration to coordination problems
**EMBRACE**: Framing collaboration as existential challenge

**AVOID**: Treating conflict as mere disagreement
**EMBRACE**: Showing how conflicts reflect ontological insecurity

**AVOID**: Presenting win-win as a framing technique
**EMBRACE**: Demonstrating win-win as relational work

### When Bridging Micro-Macro
**AVOID**: Treating levels as separate
**EMBRACE**: Showing how micro constitutes macro

**AVOID**: Simple aggregation stories
**EMBRACE**: Emergent processes and dynamics

### When Using Ethnography
**AVOID**: Description without theoretical purpose
**EMBRACE**: Every detail doing conceptual work

**AVOID**: Excessive scene-setting
**EMBRACE**: Strategic exemplars with just-enough thickness

## Quality Checks

### Before Submitting
Ask yourself:

1. **Opening**: Does it start with a compelling puzzle?
2. **Contribution**: Are all three levels (empirical, theoretical, practical) clear?
3. **Theory-Data**: Are they tightly integrated throughout?
4. **Voice**: Is it authoritative but humble?
5. **Implications**: Are they specific and challenging?
6. **Micro-Macro**: Do you bridge levels seamlessly?
7. **Relational**: Is the relational framing consistent?
8. **Accessibility**: Can intelligent non-specialists follow?

### Red Flags
- More than 2 pages before getting to your argument
- Theory section that could be from any paper
- Findings that are just descriptive
- Discussion that doesn't make bold claims
- Implications that are generic
- Levels of analysis that don't connect
- Passive voice dominating
- Excessive hedging throughout

### Green Flags
- Opening that makes reader reconsider assumptions
- Theory woven throughout empirics
- Every example doing conceptual work
- Clear, bold contribution claims
- Specific, actionable implications
- Seamless micro-macro integration
- Relational dynamics foregrounded
- Active, engaging voice
