# Writing Style Guide for Top-Tier Management Journals

## Article Architecture

### Openings
**Lead with provocative puzzle that challenges assumptions**
- Start with a counterintuitive finding or observation
- Frame the problem as paradigm-challenging, not gap-filling
- Make the reader question their existing mental models

### Framing
**Position work as paradigm-challenging, not gap-filling**
- Challenge dominant logics explicitly
- Show what current theories miss or misrepresent
- Position your contribution as necessary, not incremental

### Theory Section
**Integrate seamlessly—cite to build arguments, not display erudition**
- Avoid excessive literature review windups
- Don't list citations like credentials
- Use citations to construct a logical argument
- Engage in explicit dialogue with canonical texts

### Empirics
**Strategic exemplars with thick-enough description to theorize from**
- Every empirical example does theoretical work
- Use vivid, scene-setting narrative
- Alternate between close reading and pattern abstraction
- No purely descriptive ethnography

### Analysis
**Alternate between close reading and pattern abstraction**
- Move between granular detail and broader patterns
- Show how specific moments illuminate general principles
- Build cumulative understanding

### Discussion
**Bold, counterintuitive claims backed by evidence**
- Don't bury key insights in middle sections
- Make strong claims supported by your data
- Challenge conventional wisdom

### Implications
**Actionable for practitioners, provocative for scholars**
- Avoid generic policy implications
- Provide practical frameworks that challenge conventional wisdom
- Make findings transportable across sectors

## Sentence-Level Craft

### Paragraph Structure
- **Lead sentences carry conceptual weight**
- One key idea per paragraph
- Build cumulatively across paragraphs

### Rhythm and Variety
- Mix crisp declarative statements with complex analytical sentences
- Vary sentence length and structure
- Create a compelling reading experience

### Voice
- **Predominantly active voice**
- Use passive only when agency is genuinely diffuse
- Minimize hedging except where genuine uncertainty exists

### Strategic Pronoun Use
- "We" for inclusive, collaborative knowledge-building
- "I" for personal insight and researcher positionality
- Choose deliberately based on rhetorical purpose

## Overall Voice

**Authoritative but humble, rigorous but accessible, analytical but humanistic**

### Balance
- Confident without being arrogant
- Scholarly without being obscure
- Theoretical without losing empirical grounding
- Critical without being dismissive

### Accessibility
- Write for intelligent readers, not just specialists
- Explain complex ideas clearly
- Use jargon only when it adds precision
